51. A few months later, on the 6th of June, 1997, a paper titled "Arecibo Radar Mapping of the Lunar Poles: A Search for Ice Deposits", was published in the journal Science. Arecibo, which has as good a resolution of Clementine if not a _better_ one, didn't see any evidence for ice. 
52. "Because of the association of the high CPR areas with impact features and the location of many of these areas in sunlit regions, there is no clear evidence from the Arecibo images for the presence of ice. Most high CPR areas appear to be the result of increased surface roughness or blocky areas associated with the steep inner rim slopes of impact craters."
53. CPR just means 'Circular Polarisation Ratio'. It's a measurement of how much the signal bounces around before coming back to the receiver. The paper argued that rough terrain, not ice, was responsible for the high CPR in the Clementine data. Arecibo even received similar readings from _sunlit_ craters, where there _definitely_ couldn't be any ice.
54. On the 5th of March 1998, a press conference was held regarding the Lunar Prospector mission. NASA announced "LUNAR PROSPECTOR FINDS EVIDENCE OF ICE AT MOON'S POLES". But the actual _scientists_ were a lot more cautious saying "While the evidence of  water ice is quite strong, the water 'signal' itself is relatively weak". This seems like a contradiction ?? If the signal, which _is_ the evidence for water, is weak, then how can the evidence be strong. But of course, the media ran with the idea. On the 31st of July 1999, Prospector purposely crashed into a crater near the Lunar South Pole to try and produce signs of water that we could see. We didn't see any signs of water.
