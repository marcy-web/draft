Vitruvius, De architectura. Translated by Morris Hicky Morgan
	Eolipiles are hollow bronze balls, with a very small opening through which water is poured into them. Set before a fire, not a breath issues from them before they get warm; but as soon as they begin to boil, out comes a strong blast due to the fire. Thus from this slight and very short experiment we may understand and judge of the mighty and wonderful laws of the heavens and the nature of winds.

Aeolipiles were the world's first _steam engines_. Invented in Ancient Greece around 2,000 years ago. We don't know exactly what they were used for, but they were likely just a party trick. They show up in Hero's Pneumatica, alongside 'A bird made to whistle by flowing water' and 'An automaton, the head of which continues attached to the body, after a knife has entered the neck at one side, passed completely through it, and out at the other; which animal will drink immediately after the operation'. It wasn't practical, and it wasn't a predecessor to the steam engine that would change the world in the industrial revolution. Our rockets today are sort of in the same position aren't they? They're a party trick. A big show people come out to watch once or twice a year. They still make headline news ! They are not even _close_ to being the regular, reliable form of transportation that would make them useful. At the polar opposite end of the spectrum is the Shinkansen. A technological marvel that has had a flawless operational record for sixty years. When the Tokaido Shinkansen Line opened in 1964, it ran 60 trains every day. I don't think in our lifetimes we'll see 60 super heavy-lift launch vehicles liftoff in a single day. There are only a couple of these to have ever existed.The Saturn V launched thirteen times. Energia: twice. Falcon Heavy : twelve. SLS : two. Starship: eleven. We don't really know how many times the Long March 9 has launched but I'd wager it isn't many. And New Glenn has done it a couple times too. That's 40 something launches in 60 years. And these are _multiple_ vehicles ! We're not talking about oh, we flew the rocket up brought it back and used it again like every single other vehicle on the planet. We're talking about rebuilding _new_ launch vehicles from scratch, every time. SpaceX has promised fully reusable rocket, but they're not operational yet. And the rockets they _do_ use, are only partially reusable. These vehicles _cannot_ be produced at a cadence that allows for 60 launches a year. We just can't make them fast enough. They are a long long way away from being made assembly line style like your neighbour's Honda. 

Do you remember something else about the aeolipile? It is _not_ a predecessor to modern steam engines. This might sound a little far fetched, but I believe rockets, are the same thing. They are the aeolipile to whatever comes after them. One hundred years after launching the first liquid fuelled rocket on a snowy day in 1926, the answer to 'How do we make a better rocket?' is still 'Just do the same thing, but bigger.' This isn't really a thing with any other vehicle. An A380 isn't just a scaled up Wright Flyer y'know? If you asked them to describe a better plane, they might imagine a better engine. They would never describe a _jet_ to you. They had no frame of reference for it. This is where I believe rockets are. What will be their 'jet' moment? Their paradigm shift? That's anybody's guess. But when you do, then things _really_ open up. A whole universe of places to explore. All _way_ more exciting... than the moon.
