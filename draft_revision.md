Immediately following this announcement, on the 6th of June 1997, a paper titled "Arecibo Radar Mapping of the Lunar Poles: A Search for Ice Deposits", was published in the journal Science. The telescope, which had a resolution as good as if not _better_ than Clementine, didn't find _any_ evidence of ice. "Because of the association of the high CPR areas with impact features and the location of many of these areas in sunlit regions, there is no clear evidence from the Arecibo images for the presence of ice. Most high CPR areas appear to be the result of increased surface roughness or blocky areas associated with the steep inner rim slopes of impact craters." CPR in this case standing for 'Circular Polarisation Ratio'. A measure of how much the radio signal bounces around before coming back. Something with a low CPR, like rock, just reflects the signal back normally. Something with a _high_ CPR however, bounces the signal around multiple times before it comes back. The paper argued that rough, rocky terrain, not ice, was responsible for the high CPR in the Clementine data,and Arecibo even received similar readings from _sunlit_ craters, where ice was surely impossible.
